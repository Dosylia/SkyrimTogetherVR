# The copy holds its weapon where the owner does.
#
#   python Tools\VR\live-check.py live-weapon-grip
#
# "The hands are perfectly synced but not the swords" (2026-10-04). In VR the weapon the player sees and swings hangs
# off the first-person hand, at the angle VR holds it; the copy on the other screen hung its own off its third-person
# hand, the skeleton's way. The sender now reads the first-person attach node relative to its body's hand and sends it
# (VRPose::HasWeapons), and the receiver puts the copy's weapon there (VRBodySync::PlaceWeapons).
#
# The first-person hands are read at PlayerCharacter+0x590/0x598, offsets taken from CommonLibVR-NG and not measured
# here: the first check is that the node there is the hand. The player then draws an iron sword, the bot records the
# player's stream and plays it back as its own character, and the game places the bot's copy's weapon from it.
log live weapon grip
waitfor connected 45
expect known me
waitfor known other 40
wait 3

log DO console player.additem 12EB7 1
log DO console player.equipitem 12EB7
wait 2
log DO papyrus Actor DrawWeapon 14
wait 4

# In view of the headset (see live-hands), or the copy is not posed at all.
log DO papyrus ObjectReference MoveTo 14 copy f:50 f:0 f:0 b:false
wait 3
log -- recording the player's stream for 12 s
capture other 12
wait 1
handtargets -15 15 70 15 20 80
replay me 3
wait 3

# "first-person right hand found at PlayerCharacter+0x598" is written once, at the first pose after connecting --
# before this script begins -- so it is not a check here; without the hand there is no "weapon held" line at all.
log CHECK logged local right weapon held \d+ degrees
log CHECK logged weapon placed where its owner holds it
log CHECK hands within 80
log SHOT grip of copy
wait 4
log CHECK game alive
wait 3
log done
stop
